Objectives & Materials

Course Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

  • Critically evaluate publicly available health and social data to identify disparities in maternal and child health, gun violence, intimate partner violence, opioid use, suicide and self-harm, and adverse childhood experiences.
  • Apply intermediate-to-advanced R skills (tidyverse, sf, ggplot2, tidytext, Shiny) and QGIS to analyze and visualize community-level health data.
  • Conduct spatial analysis including buffer analysis, spatial joins, and Local Moran’s I to identify geographic patterns of health inequity.
  • Explain the legal, physical, and social determinants of health and articulate how policy shapes population health outcomes.
  • Interpret and critically assess the role of historical policies (e.g., redlining, exclusionary zoning) in producing present-day health disparities.
  • Evaluate the ethical and legal implications of big data use in public health, including algorithmic bias, privacy, and data sovereignty.
  • Translate complex quantitative findings into actionable policy briefs and stakeholder presentations for non-technical audiences.
  • Collaborate with community partners to co-design data-driven public health recommendations.

Competencies

This course addresses the following College of Public Health competencies. A complete list is located at cph.osu.edu/students/competencies.

BSPH Foundational Competencies

  • Apply the fundamental principles of the five core disciplines of public health to domestic and international population issues (1)
  • Communicate public health information, in both oral and written forms, through a variety of media and to diverse audiences (8)
  • Locate, use, evaluate and synthesize public health information (9)

BSPH Public Health Methods Specialization

  • Identify strengths and weaknesses of specific approaches to collecting health-related data (2)
  • Evaluate the strength of evidence from various study designs used in public health research (3)
  • Interpret scientific evidence to assess how various influences affect health outcomes at both the individual and population levels (4)

MPH Foundational Competencies

  • Apply epidemiological methods to settings and situations in public health practice (1)
  • Select quantitative and qualitative data collection methods appropriate for a given public health context (2)
  • Analyze quantitative and qualitative data using biostatistics, informatics, computer-based programming and software (3)
  • Interpret results of data analysis for public health research, policy or practice (4)
  • Discuss the means by which structural bias, social inequities and racism undermine health (6)
  • Assess population needs, assets and capacities that affect communities’ health (7)
  • Design a population-based policy, program, project or intervention (9)
  • Select methods to evaluate public health programs (11)
  • Discuss the policy-making process, including the roles of ethics and evidence (12)
  • Advocate for political, social or economic policies and programs that will improve health (14)
  • Communicate audience-appropriate public health content (19)
  • Apply systems thinking tools to visually represent a public health issue (22)

Required Texts and Readings

There is no single required textbook. All readings are drawn from peer-reviewed literature, government reports, and policy documents available via Carmen or open access online.

Class Format

Mode of Delivery: In-person, two meetings per week: one 75-minute lecture/discussion session and one 75-minute Classroom Lab Demonstration.

Weekly Lab System: During the Classroom Lab Demonstration, the instructor walks through the week’s analysis live in R. Students then complete a self-graded take-home R Markdown document posted on Carmen. Students knit the completed document to HTML and submit via Carmen by Sunday 11:59 PM.

Credit Hours & Work Expectations: Expect ~3 hours per week of direct instruction and ~6 hours of homework/active learning activities.

Attendance: Attendance is expected at all lecture and Classroom Lab Demonstration sessions. More than three unexcused absences will affect your participation grade.

Course Technology

  • Computer: Mac (OSX) or PC (Windows 10+) with high-speed internet.
  • Software: R (v4.3+) and RStudio Desktop (free). QGIS (v3.x+) (free).
  • Accounts: GitHub account (free) for version control and portfolio submission. Microsoft 365 Copilot (free for OSU students).
